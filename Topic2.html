            <h2>Subtopic 2: Modern HTTP/3 and the QUIC Protocol</h2>

            <p>Even with HTTP/2 performance gains, packet loss at the transport layer (TCP) could still stall all multiplexed streams. HTTP/3 replaces TCP entirely with <strong>QUIC</strong> (Quick UDP Internet Connections).</p>

            

            <div class="highlight-box">

                <h3>Why UDP over TCP?</h3>

                <p>QUIC operates on top of UDP, allowing individual streams to handle their own error recovery independently. If a packet drops on Stream A, Stream B can continue processing without disruption.</p>

            </div>


            <h3>Core Benefits of HTTP/3</h3>

            <ul>

                <li><strong>Zero-RTT Connection Setup:</strong> Speeds up handshake procedures for returning clients.</li>

                <li><strong>Connection Migration:</strong> Seamlessly maintains sessions when users switch networks (e.g., Wi-Fi to cellular data).</li>

                <li><strong>Built-in Encryption:</strong> TLS 1.3 is integrated natively into the protocol framework.</li>

            </ul>
